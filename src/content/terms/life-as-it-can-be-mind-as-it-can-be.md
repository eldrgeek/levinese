The research posture that present-day organisms and familiar minds are only a small sample of what life and cognition can be. Levin uses synthetic biology, xenobots, anthrobots, chimeras, AI, and hybrid systems as ways to explore a wider possibility space rather than merely cataloging natural history. Life-as-it-can-be asks what living matter can do when its normal constraints are changed; mind-as-it-can-be asks what kinds of agency, memory, goal-seeking, and communication can appear in unfamiliar embodiments.
    <br><br>
    The phrase matters because it links Levin's practical bioengineering to his broader theory of diverse intelligence. A novel biological construct is not just a tool or curiosity. It is an experiment on the boundaries of life, mind, and embodiment: a way to ask which competencies were latent in the material all along.
