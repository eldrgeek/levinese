Levin's operational use of goal-directedness means the capacity of a system to reduce the gap between its current state and a preferred state by sensing, acting, and correcting across perturbations. It does not require human-like intention, verbal planning, or conscious desire. A homeostatic loop, a regenerating limb, a wound-healing tissue, and an animal solving a behavioral task can all be compared by the scale and flexibility of the goals they pursue.
    <br><br>
    This is why Levin treats teleology as an engineering variable rather than a forbidden metaphysical word. A process is more goal-directed when it can reach the same outcome from varied starting points, compensate for obstacles, and stop when the target is achieved. The question becomes empirical: what model of the system gives the best prediction and control?
