A sudden rise in a cell's calcium level, often released from internal stores or let in from outside, can trigger movement, changes in gene activity, and rearrangement of the cell's structure. Calcium ions (Ca²⁺) serve as a versatile intracellular messenger in virtually all eukaryotic cells. Transient rises in cytoplasmic Ca²⁺ are triggered by IP₃-mediated release from the endoplasmic reticulum or by Ca²⁺ entry through plasma membrane channels. These transients activate kinases, transcription factors, cytoskeletal rearrangements, and cell motility programs — a nearly universal cellular alarm-and-mobilization system.
    <br><br>
    <strong>Levin's lens:</strong> In Levin's framework, calcium signals are not only metabolic triggers — they carry information across cell collectives. Calcium waves propagating through gap junctions relay error signals and stress over tissue distances, functioning as a form of cognitive glue. The embryo teratogen-resistance work (Tung et al. 2024) extends this beyond the single body: embryos exposed to a teratogen in a large cohort survive better, and with fewer defects, than embryos exposed alone. Read that result precisely — the inter-embryo assistance is mediated by short-range <em>diffusible</em> signalling involving the P2 ATP receptor, so what passes from embryo to embryo is extracellular ATP, not calcium. Calcium is the receiving end of that exchange: the intracellular transient and the intra-tissue wave that the ATP signal triggers inside each embryo. The two scales are different media — ATP diffusing through the shared medium between bodies, calcium travelling through gap junctions within a body — and the collective repair response is computational rather than merely chemical because of how those signals are integrated, not because calcium itself crosses between embryos. Calcium signalling is therefore one medium through which a tissue's bioelectric state becomes an inter-cellular language, routing information about anatomical error and stress from where it arises to where it can be acted on.
